Overall Experience

Overall, it kind of felt like a used-car salesman, lol. It kept asking me for things I did not think it needed to ask for. I wanted a recommendation and help finishing the job, rather than having to keep figuring out the next step myself. The specialists did useful partial work, but I am still not sure whether that saved me time overall. The final resolved statuses also do not mean that all three agent runs succeeded. Failed runs, later human approvals, and simulated fulfillment need to be evaluated separately.

I expected Boss to start with Inventory, bring in Accounting for the overdue invoice and shared cash, check rent timing with Facilities, and use Customer Service for a truthful reply. Inventory and Accounting did check the important facts: CC-TEE-WHITE, size S, had zero stock, and invoice 501 was an overdue $840 obligation. Accounting produced an exact payment proposal. However, Boss failed before returning a validated final result, and the expected Facilities check and Customer Service draft did not complete in that run. The audit records AGENT_EXECUTION_FAILED, but the available safe logs do not establish the specific underlying cause. I would not guess why it failed.

The invoice proposal was recovered from Accounting's completed structured result and later paid once after my approval. That is useful work, but it was not a completed customer-order workflow. I wanted the system to suggest how to finish after Boss failed. The ticket was eventually resolved through separately approved simulation steps using the simulated date 2026-09-07: receipt of one tee changed stock from 0 to 1, and a simulated fulfillment adjustment changed it from 1 to 0. The source was explicitly human-provided simulation input, not verified supplier evidence. Actual therefore differed from Expected: the specialists checked facts and prepared a payment, while later human-approved simulation actions finished the recorded outcome.

I thought it should just wait for tokens to refresh and keep going on its own. That was my expectation, not a feature the app supported. This limit was a cumulative run budget, not a time-based provider allowance. Waiting would not replenish it, and the backend did not automatically resume failed runs. A fresh approved run or resume would make additional model requests. The payment preparation matched Expected, but the agent loop did not finish the verification and closure on its own.

Five Agents Versus One Agent With Tools

I would stick with five agents. The specialists got parts of the work done even when the whole workflow did not finish, especially Accounting's recoverable invoice and rent proposals. However, I do not know whether five agents were faster or better than one agent with tools because we did not compare them.

One agent could potentially simplify the handoffs by reading the same ticket, stock, invoice, lease, and cash tools, then producing one structured recommendation, payment proposal, and board draft. That might reduce repeated context and the need for Boss to collect specialist replies after useful work was already done. This is a possible simplification, not a tested result. It would still need exact human approval for writes and would still have to distinguish simulated stock movements from actual delivery.

Three New Problems the Existing Tools Could Help With

These examples came up during brainstorming, and I picked them. First, the team could flag low stock and make a reorder list for specified SKU-size pairs. Inventory can use get_product_snapshot and compare quantities with a human-provided reorder cutoff. The tee and hoodie shortages show why these checks matter. Checking the whole shop automatically would need an inventory-list tool and stored thresholds. The current purchase tool can record an exactly approved order when valid supplier and quote details are supplied, but obtaining those details automatically would need supplier access. A reorder list is not an order or delivered stock.

Second, Customer Service could draft order-status updates from get_ticket_context, get_product_snapshot, and the invoice or purchase records. Ticket 103 shows the kind of grounded board draft it can produce. It must say when shipment or handoff information is missing. Sending updates would need a messaging tool and authorization, and the current assignment keeps messages as drafts on the board.

Third, Accounting could flag upcoming supplier bills for known invoice IDs and prepare payments for review using get_invoice_details, get_invoice_payment_details, and get_cash_snapshot. The rent-context tool also supplies the simulated shop date for judging dates. Invoice 501 shows that the team can identify an overdue obligation and produce an exact payment proposal. Automatic reminders across all bills would additionally need an invoice-list or due-date search tool and a scheduler. Human approval would still be required for payment.

Three Problems the Current Tools Could Not Fully Solve

First, the team could not spot damaged merchandise on a shelf. The inventory tools report recorded quantities and prices, not physical condition. It would need a person to inspect the items or a camera system that could reliably detect damage, plus a tool to record inspection results. Inventory or a quality-control agent could coordinate that work, but an extra agent alone would not provide the missing evidence.

Second, it could not actually hand an item to a customer. That needs a worker or delivery service and a reliable handoff or delivery-confirmation record. A fulfillment agent could coordinate those steps. Ticket 101 makes the distinction clear: recording stock 0 to 1 to 0 and resolving simulated fulfillment is not the same as delivering the tee.

Third, it could not figure out where a missing package went. It would need carrier tracking, a tracking reference tied to the order, and possibly someone to investigate or open a claim. A logistics agent could use those additional tools. The existing vendor and receipt records do not locate a package, and the simulated receipts do not prove a supplier shipped anything. More agents could help coordinate these problems, but they would still need the tools or people that the current app lacks.
